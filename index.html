Margin-Aware Residual Reinforcement Learning for Pre-Harvest Reaching in Deformable Fruit Canopies

MarginReach learns contact-rich pre-harvest reaching by combining a behavior-cloned IK prior with residual PPO guided by normalized contact-force and branch-deflection utilization.

Abstract

Using compliant branches without letting contact become overload.

Dense fruit canopies rarely admit a strictly collision-free approach path. Treating every branch as a rigid obstacle is overly conservative, but optimizing only reaching success can drive policies to push through vegetation with excessive contact force or branch deflection.

MarginReach makes this trade-off explicit. It maps arm-link contact forces and branch-joint deflections to normalized utilization signals, then uses those signals to train a residual Cartesian policy on top of a behavior-cloned reaching prior. The result is a controller that can exploit mild branch compliance while reducing contact-force and branch-deflection overloads.

Contributions

From binary collision avoidance to margin-regulated canopy interaction.

The current manuscript frames pre-harvest reaching as regulated physical interaction with deformable branches, not as strict obstacle avoidance.

C1

Normalized utilization interface

Manipulator contact forces and branch-joint deflections are converted into continuous proximity-to-limit indicators for robot-contact and plant-damage risk.

C2

Margin-aware residual policy

A behavior-cloned IK prior supplies nominal reaching motion, while PPO learns reactive Cartesian corrections conditioned on contact utilization.

C3

Simulation and hardware protocol

Held-out procedural canopies and 30 real-robot trials evaluate success, safe success, contact overload, and branch overload separately.

Method

Margin utilization turns physical interaction into a graded learning signal.

Instead of treating contact as a binary collision, MarginReach measures how close robot links and branches are to their admissible limits.

01

Compliant procedural canopy

L-system tree assets are converted into articulated branch structures with spring-actuated joints, fruit targets, and randomized obstacle-tree clutter.

02

Force and deflection utilization

Contact forces and branch deflections are normalized into link-wise and joint-wise proximity-to-limit indicators for reward, termination, and evaluation.

03

Prior-guided residual PPO

A frozen BC/IK reaching prior provides smooth nominal motion, while PPO learns local Cartesian residuals that react to canopy interaction severity.

Contact utilization uᶜᵢ = min(Fᵢ / Fᶜʳⁱᵗᵢ, 1)

Link-wise force readings become normalized contact-risk signals before hard overloads occur.

Branch utilization uᵇⱼ = min(|qⱼ − qʳᵉᶠⱼ| / qˡⁱᵐⱼ, 1)

Branch deflection is measured relative to the settled resting angle and bending-limit threshold.

Residual action aₜ = πBC(oₜ) + Δπθ(oₜ)

The residual controller starts near the imitation prior and learns local contact-aware corrections.

Results

MarginReach improves safe success in simulation and hardware.

Evaluation separates nominal task completion from mechanically safe completion using SR, SSR, contact-overload rate, and branch-overload rate.

SR Success Rate

End-effector reaches the pre-harvest band with the required approach orientation.

SSR Safe Success Rate

Success while all robot-link contact forces and branch deflections stay within limits.

COR Contact-Overload Rate

Any monitored arm link reaches or exceeds its force-utilization limit.

BOR Branch-Overload Rate

Any fruit-tree or obstacle-tree branch exceeds its utilization limit.

Ablation Study

Safe success depends on both the residual structure and margin penalties.

The manuscript separates learning-framework variants from margin-utilization variants to show where the safe interaction behavior comes from.

The full policy intentionally trades a small amount of nominal SR for the best SSR and the lowest overload rates, matching the paper's emphasis on mechanically safe pre-harvest reaching.
